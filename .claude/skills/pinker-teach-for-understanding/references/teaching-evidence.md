# Evidence and attribution for Pinker-informed teaching

Research current through 2026-09-03.

## Provenance classes

- **Direct Pinker advice:** Pinker explicitly recommends the practice.
- **Observed Pinker practice:** It appears in his course, syllabus, lecture, or explanatory work; this is not causal evidence of effectiveness.
- **Workflow inference:** A restrained design implication assembled from his work.
- **Independent evidence:** Educational or cognitive research that supports an extension but should not be credited to Pinker.
- **Boundary:** A limitation, disputed extension, or attribution that must not be made.

## Direct teaching evidence

In a 2014 Harvard Gazette interview, Pinker says great teaching begins with passion for the subject; avoids “professional narcissism” about disciplinary guilds, fashions, and buzzwords; attends to the curse of knowledge; obtains real feedback from exams, office hours, discussion, and questions; and revises accordingly. He frames students and readers as smart and curious but less knowledgeable, and he treats writing instruction as iterative revision rather than one-pass performance.

Source: [Harvard Gazette, “What could be more interesting than how the mind works?”](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2014/05/what-could-be-more-interesting-than-how-the-mind-works/)

A 2003 profile records Pinker's emphasis on breaking down and systematizing difficult concepts, analogies that reach the heart of a concept, and giving undergraduates a defensible current conclusion rather than drowning them in the full archaeology of failed experiments. This is reflective advice, not an experimental comparison of methods.

Source: [“Mind of a Teacher,” archived by Pinker](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/mind_of_a_teacher_2003_06_24_guardian.pdf)

## Observed course design

The 2020 *Rationality* syllabus moves from normative tools—logic, probability, Bayesian inference, decision theory, game theory, and critical thinking—to cognitive biases and then to real-world applications. Lectures and readings are combined with discussion, weekly problems, examples from news and everyday life, cumulative assessment, and a capstone applying the tools to a consequential problem.

Its study advice emphasizes active organization, retrieval without the text open, explanation in one's own words, spacing, discussion, and deep understanding rather than familiarity with words. The syllabus recommends handwritten notes, but the stronger portable principle is generative, organized note-making; do not impose one medium universally.

Sources: [Harvard Rationality course](https://stevenpinker.com/classes/rationality-gened-1066) and [2020 syllabus](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/rationality_syllabus_2020.pdf)

## Independent evidence used in the synthesis

The term “curse of knowledge” originated in experiments on asymmetric information, not in Pinker's work. Pinker later applied and popularized it for communication.

Source: [Camerer, Loewenstein, and Weber, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1086/261651)

Across four experiments, Tullis and Feder found that newly acquired knowledge impaired people's estimates of what novices knew, and mere instructions to reduce reliance on one's own experience did not eliminate the problem. This supports diagnostic evidence about learners over introspection alone.

Source: [Tullis and Feder, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-022-01382-3)

Cognitive-load research supports adjusting interacting elements to prior schemas: what an expert processes as one chunk may require many linked elements for a novice.

Source: [Sweller, van Merriënboer, and Paas, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5)

Meta-analytic and review evidence supports authentic problems, dialogue, mentoring, low-stakes retrieval, and informative task feedback, with important heterogeneity across contexts. These are independent extensions, not “Pinker's method.”

Sources: [Abrami et al. on critical thinking](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314551063), [Agarwal, Nunes, and Blunt on retrieval practice](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9), and [Wisniewski, Zierer, and Hattie on feedback](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087)

Expert blind spot has domain-specific support in mathematics instruction; do not universalize the effect size or intervention from a single domain.

Source: [Nathan and Petrosino, 2003](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312040004905)

Teacher enthusiasm is linked more securely to motivation and perception than to guaranteed achievement. Keep the outcome claim modest.

Source: [Keller et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9354-y)

## Safe attribution language

Use language such as:

> Pinker directly recommends testing explanations on real readers or learners because expertise hides missing steps. Diagnostic prequestions are a research-supported extension, not a procedure experimentally tested by Pinker.

Reject claims that this is “Pinker’s pedagogy,” that his course is an efficacy trial, that concrete stories should replace abstractions, or that clarity alone proves learning.
