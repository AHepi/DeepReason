# Teaching patterns

Use these structures selectively. Do not force every output into every field.

## Compact lesson design

### Learner model

Record the intended audience, likely prior knowledge, misconceptions, accessibility needs, time, and assumptions that still require testing.

### Destination

Complete: “Afterward, the learner can ___ when given ___ without ___.”

### Explanatory model

State the phenomenon, central claim, mechanism or logical chain, required vocabulary, concrete case, scope, and uncertainty.

### Learner moves

Select a prediction before instruction, an explain-back, a near-miss comparison, a novel application, or a critique. Make the learner generate an answer before revealing it when appropriate.

### Evidence of learning

Specify what a strong answer must contain, what a plausible misconception looks like, and how the next explanation changes for each outcome.

## Live tutoring loop

1. Pose one diagnostic question that reveals the learner's model.
2. Interpret the reasoning, not only the final answer.
3. Supply the smallest missing link, example, or distinction.
4. Ask the learner to reconstruct or use the idea.
5. Increase variation or difficulty only after the model holds.
6. End with a delayed or transferable check the learner can attempt later.

Do not answer every self-generated question in a long lecture when the setting permits interaction.

## Explanation arc

Use this sentence-level skeleton when useful:

> Here is the phenomenon. The tempting first account is ___. It misses ___. Consider this concrete case. The governing idea is ___. It works because first ___, then ___, which implies ___. It applies when ___ and does not establish ___. Try it on this new case: ___.

## Analogy test

Before using an analogy, identify:

- the target concept;
- the familiar source;
- the relevant correspondence between their parts or relations;
- the inference the analogy is meant to support;
- the first important way the analogy breaks.

Reject analogies that are memorable but structurally irrelevant.

## Teaching audit

Check in this order:

| Question | Failure signal | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the model correct? | False or unsupported claim | Repair content and evidence before style |
| Is the destination observable? | “Know” or “understand” with no performance | Specify explain, predict, distinguish, apply, or critique |
| Does the lesson expose the phenomenon? | Opens with administrative or disciplinary labels | Begin with the object, puzzle, or consequence |
| Are prerequisites visible? | Acronyms, hidden premises, unexplained expert chunks | Define, decompose, reorder, or defer |
| Do examples teach the abstraction? | Vivid story with no mapped point | State the mapping and return to the principle |
| Must the learner reason? | Listening and recognition only | Add generation, retrieval, comparison, or application |
| Is feedback diagnostic? | Generic praise, grades, or assent | Ask for paraphrase, reasoning, error, and transfer evidence |
| Is transfer tested? | Only repeats the demonstrated form | Change surface features and withhold the tool name |
| Is the method represented honestly? | All choices attributed to Pinker | Separate direct, observed, derived, and external support |

## Asynchronous explanation

When no live learner is present, include a short self-test after the explanation. Put answers far enough away that they do not spoil retrieval. State that the lesson has not received representative-learner feedback and suggest the most informative pilot question.
