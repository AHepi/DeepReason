# Revision diagnostics

Use the lightest process that fits the request. A short email does not need a full ledger; a high-stakes report may.

## Diagnostic sequence

| Pass | Ask | Common symptom | Typical repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Truth | Is every material claim warranted and scoped? | False precision, unsupported fluency, missing exception | Verify, qualify, cite, or remove |
| Topic and point | Can the reader say what this is about and what it says? | Process-first opening, buried claim | Lead with phenomenon, problem, or point |
| Global order | Does each section prepare the next? | Writer's discovery order, repeated resets | Reorder by cause, time, comparison, or problem-solution |
| Coherence | Is the relation between adjacent claims recoverable? | Abrupt jump or ambiguous “this” | Stabilize terms, add relation, repair referent |
| Knowledge gap | What only an insider can supply? | Acronym, jargon, hidden premise, floating example | Define, unpack, connect, or concretize |
| Syntax | Can the sentence be parsed once from left to right? | Garden path, remote verb, ambiguous attachment | Reorder given/new and light/heavy; restructure |
| Voice and action | Are agency and topic placed intentionally? | Hidden responsibility or needless actor switching | Choose active/passive for discourse function |
| Compression | Does every phrase earn its load? | Throat-clearing, repetition, cliché, zombie noun | Cut or turn abstraction into actor and action |
| Usage | Is this an error, register choice, convention, or taste? | Folklore correction | Consult evidence and applicable style |
| Sound | Does reading aloud reveal friction? | Backtracking, accidental emphasis, monotonous rhythm | Rebalance syntax and cadence |

## Reader-comprehension probes

Ask a representative reader to answer:

1. What is the main claim in your own words?
2. What does each technical term refer to?
3. Where did you first reread or hesitate?
4. How does this example support the general point?
5. Which statement is evidence, which is inference, and which is qualification?
6. What action, decision, or prediction follows?

Do not replace these probes with a readability score. Sentence and word length can flag passages for inspection but cannot establish comprehension.

## Material edit ledger

When the user requests explanations of edits, record only changes worth their attention:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Priority | Required, recommended, or optional |
| Class | Fact, meaning, coherence, knowledge gap, syntax, usage, register, or rhythm |
| Reader symptom | Misunderstanding, missing context, backtrack, ambiguity, excess load, or expectation mismatch |
| Change | Compact before → after description |
| Rationale | Context-specific principle, not a slogan |
| Fidelity | Whether meaning, confidence, voice, or technical precision changed |
| Alternative | Another defensible version when relevant |

Reserve **required** for factual errors, genuine ambiguity, actual grammar violations, safety or legal obligations, and binding house-style rules. Most style improvements are **recommended** or **optional**.

## Teaching exercise sequence

1. Show a short model or contrast.
2. Ask the learner to describe the reader effect before naming the rule.
3. Explain the cognitive or rhetorical principle.
4. Give a constrained rewrite that isolates the choice.
5. Test the result by paraphrase or first-read processing.
6. Ask the learner to defend the choice and name an alternative context where another form would work better.

Useful exercises include comparing classic, procedural, legal, and oratorical stances; grounding a nominalized paragraph in visible actors; pairing every generalization with an example; choosing active versus passive while tracking the topic; reassembling shuffled propositions by their coherence relations; and adjudicating a disputed usage rule by grammar, register, convention, and taste.
