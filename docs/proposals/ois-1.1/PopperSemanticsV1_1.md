# PopperSemantics

## Version 1.1

## A recursive semantic theory of explanatory knowledge and universal explanatory capacity

### Purpose

The subject of this theory is how a system can create explanations, expose errors in them, and change the problems, standards, and methods through which it does that. Its target is **explanatory universality**: an organization whose explanatory reach is not permanently confined to a predetermined subject matter, vocabulary, repertoire of answers, or hierarchy of judges.

The kernel is recursive. Recognizing a problem can itself present a problem. Inventing a conjecture, understanding a criticism, interpreting an observation, choosing between explanations, or deciding whether a standard is appropriate can each require the same process of conjecture and criticism. The process can return to any of those activities without installing a final authority above them. This is the substantive structure described in *The Fabric of Reality*, Chapter 3, particularly printed pages 67–68. [F3]

This is a semantic theory, not an execution procedure. No algorithm for generating explanations, computing verdicts, recognizing every error, or completing an inquiry is required. Whether a particular physical realization is computable is neither a premise nor a conclusion here. Recursive self-application must not be reduced to a computational fixed point, and it does not, merely by being recursive, establish a mathematical uncomputability result.

The title names a proposed Popperian reconstruction, informed by Deutsch and Marletto. It does not attribute the complete theory below to any of them. Source-backed commitments, additional modeling commitments, definitions, and derived consequences are distinguished throughout. Source keys are resolved at the end. This document is the semantic authority for specifications that explicitly adopt this version. Authority here fixes the meanings against which a specification is assessed; it does not confer immunity from criticism on the theory.

# Part I-A. The explanatory core

## Reality is not conferred by a judgment — K-REAL

An explanation attempts to say how something is, works, or follows from a structure, and why the relevant phenomenon or relation has the character it does. Its content is not exhausted by the observations it fits, the responses it produces, or the approval it receives. A represented explanation, an agent’s assessment of it, and what it actually explains are different things. This distinction applies to an assessment of the present theory as much as to any other assessment. [F1; B1]

Errors can be real even when nobody has identified them. An objection can be mistaken even when nobody has answered it. No authority, procedure, record, tradition, or successful history turns a fallible judgment into a guarantee. Fallibility does not imply that all explanations are equally good or that knowledge is impossible. It means that explanatory improvement is not final certification. [F3; B1]

## A problem is an interpreted difficulty that matters — K-PROBLEM

An active problem arises when a system recognizes a tension, inadequacy, or unresolved relation among ideas and directs attention to it as worth addressing. The difficulty may be inexplicit. It need not be a sentence of the form “P and not P,” an experimental anomaly, or an emergency. A wish to understand something more deeply can be enough. Recognition and values affect which difficulty receives attention; neither makes the recognized difficulty automatically genuine. [F3; B1]

The world does not supply a ready-made partition into the problems a thinker must solve. Background explanations, recognition, purposes, and standards contribute to that partition. Each can subsequently become problematic. Attention therefore belongs inside the theory rather than being an unexplained selector outside it.

## Conjecture precedes criticism of that conjecture — K-CONJECTURE

A conjecture supplies a proposed content, interpretation, or organization to criticize. It need not be justified before it is entertained. Observation may occasion its invention, and existing knowledge may contribute to it, but neither repetition nor deduction from observational instances supplies the missing explanatory content by itself. [F3; B1]

The order is a dependence of roles: criticism has something it criticizes. It is not a requirement that every conjecture be written out before a person can notice a difficulty. An inexplicit expectation can be the prior conjecture. A criticism is also conjectural content; once proposed, it too can be criticized. “Conjecture before criticism” does not appoint an uncriticizable first conjecture.

## Criticism has a reason-bearing role — K-CRITICISM

Criticism proposes that some content, inference, application, standard, or practice has a defect relevant to a problem. A reason-sensitive response depends on what the objection says and on how the alleged defect bears on the problem. Negative feedback, punishment, disagreement, failure, or rejection is not criticism merely because it changes behavior. Such events can become evidentially significant through an interpretation that connects them to a conjecture and an alleged error. [F3; B7]

A response may improve an explanation, reject it, retain it against an objection, investigate further, reinterpret evidence, alter a standard, reframe the problem, or suspend judgment. It can also be mistaken. The semantic role of a critical response and its epistemic success must remain separate.

## The same kernel applies to its own working parts — K-RECURSION

Anything doing explanatory or adjudicative work is eligible to become the subject of further conjecture and criticism. This includes a problem formulation, a criticism, the interpretation of a reason, an inferential connection, an observation model, a comparison, a novelty attribution, a system boundary, a stopping decision, an evidential practice, and the methods being used to assess them. [F3]

Recursive scrutiny must be able to matter. A criticism of a standard can change how arguments using that standard are assessed. A criticism of an interpretation can change what an earlier utterance is taken to have said. A criticism of an attribution boundary can change who receives credit. Merely allowing comments about a method while permanently insulating its consequences would not satisfy this commitment.

Recursion does not require an infinite chain of prior authorizations. A system can reason provisionally with its present resources while examining a particular part of them. It may suspend the examination, act under a qualified judgment, or discover a better starting point. No requirement to solve every subsidiary problem first is imposed. The possibility of further criticism is not an obligation to remain forever in the same episode.

## Creation has a provenance; understanding can be reconstructive — K-ORIGIN

Attribution of creativity concerns the production or reconstruction of problem-specific explanatory organization, not merely the emission of an unfamiliar token. A prior explanation can contribute to a new creative act. A learner can reconstruct an explanation that already exists elsewhere without having possessed that understanding beforehand. A group can create through contributions that no isolated participant supplies in full. [F1; B7; B16]

Newness, authorship, understanding, correctness, and historical priority are therefore distinct. A communicated explanation is not automatically a disqualification, and an absence of a communicated complete answer is not automatically proof of authorship.

## Knowledge growth is explanatory improvement, not persistence — K-PROGRESS

Successful explanatory knowledge creation requires an actual improvement in the relevant problem situation through explanatory work and error correction. A new conjecture need not achieve it. A critical response need not achieve it. Retention alone does not achieve it. Conversely, improvement need not consist in producing a final replacement theory: exposing a defective question, correcting a standard, or establishing a principled limitation can improve the situation. [F3]

The resulting knowledge remains fallible and can generate new problems. Improvement is assessed with its scope and dependencies visible. Changing the standard to declare victory does not establish improvement; neither does requiring every old commitment to survive, including commitments that the criticism has shown to be mistaken.

## Universality is a capacity claim, not a collection of successful episodes — K-UNIVERSALITY

The target universal class contains systems capable, under suitable non-question-begging enabling conditions, of understanding explanations and conducting creative, critical inquiry across explanatory subject matters without a fixed parochial barrier. The capacity includes extending the represented vocabulary, revising problem formulations, and improving the methods needed for unfamiliar domains. [B6; B7; B16]

This is stronger than an ability to repeat criticism, stronger than performance across a large finite menu, and stronger than permission to discuss anything. It is also different from already knowing everything, succeeding on every attempt, solving a physically impossible demand, possessing unlimited resources now, or surviving indefinitely. Its formal obligations appear in Part II and are constitutive of the universal class, not deferred implementation work.

K-UNIVERSALITY is an explicit substantive target commitment. It is not derived from K-RECURSION alone. Whether a particular system possesses that capacity requires an explanatory account of its organization; a finite performance record does not logically settle the question by itself.

## Physical embodiment constrains without replacing explanation — K-PHYSICALITY

When this theory is applied to an actual system, its contents and activities must have a physically tenable realization. Physical embodiment does not make semantic organization dispensable. An account of particle trajectories and an account of why a theory was revised answer different questions, although they must be compatible. [F1]

Constructor theory contributes a language of possible and impossible transformations, information-bearing substrates, and retained capacities. It does not, just by calling a process a constructor, explain the origin of its knowledge or the meaning of its representations. Physical knowledge that contributes to its own preservation and explanatory knowledge that improves understanding are related but nonidentical notions. [M5; M7]

# Part I-B. Why the independent commitments are needed

## What is being justified

The following arguments explain why these commitments are adopted and which explanatory distinctions would be lost without them. They are not claims of ultimate justification. Nor are they proofs that no alternative formalism could use a different primitive vocabulary. A commitment is independent here when it is not supplied merely by the other displayed definitions and must be stated rather than smuggled into a theorem.

## Explanatory realism cannot be supplied by a record of endorsements

Two communities can endorse the same sentence for different reasons, or endorse incompatible sentences. Recording endorsements determines what they accepted, not which account explains the phenomenon. Omitting K-REAL permits a model in which “good explanation” means only “accepted by the selected judge.” The record can then be impeccable while its epistemology is authoritarian. Realism provides the independent distinction between being judged adequate and being adequate in the stated respect. It does not provide an infallible test of that distinction. [F1; B1]

## Problem recognition cannot be supplied by an external task label

A thermostat, a student, and an investigator can all be exposed to a temperature reading. Describing the input as a “problem” does not establish that each system recognizes the same difficulty, or any difficulty. K-PROBLEM requires an interpretation of the role the discrepancy plays for the system. Values explain why attention goes to this difficulty rather than indefinitely many other differences. This is a separate burden from explaining a response once a problem has been identified. The detailed recognition-and-attention formulation is a modeling commitment motivated by the source account of problem selection. [F3]

## Conjectural origin is not a consequence of causal dependence on data

A system may causally depend on observations without extracting an explanation from them. The same readings can be interpreted through different accounts of the instruments, circumstances, and relevant regularities. K-CONJECTURE prevents a causal input relation from becoming an undeclared rule of epistemic instruction. It leaves room for observation, mathematical deduction, analogy, imagination, and inherited knowledge to participate in a creative process without making any one of them an automatic source of explanatory warrant. [F3; B1]

## Criticism is not supplied by variation and selection alone

Differential persistence can occur without a represented objection. Conversely, a person can understand an objection and decide, for reasons, not to make the proposed change. K-CRITICISM therefore adds a content-sensitive relation that cannot be recovered merely by counting revisions or selected outputs. Its causal analysis requires substantive interpretation. A declaration that a signal was a “reason” is insufficient. [F3; B7]

## Recursion is stronger than maintaining an attackable archive

An archive can contain criticism of a standard while the institution using the archive continues to apply that standard unconditionally. K-RECURSION requires a possible return from scrutiny to the operative use of the target. This adds something beyond the existence of criticism tokens. It also blocks a fixed hierarchy in which the final judge’s judgments are the sole objects that can never acquire a defect. The source’s account of subsidiary problem solving supports the general commitment; its precise representation below is an additional semantic construction. [F3]

## Provenance is not supplied by output quality

An answer can be accurate because its organization was created by the system, supplied by a collaborator, or retrieved from a prepared store. Conversely, a communicated answer may still require the recipient to create a new understanding. K-ORIGIN locates the explanatory contribution rather than inferring it from surface quality or excluding it from a transmission history. [B7; B16]

## Improvement is not supplied by either change or retention

Changing a theory may make it worse; retaining a theory may preserve an error. Even removing a real local defect can damage the explanation elsewhere. K-PROGRESS adds the relation between the contribution and the whole relevant problem situation. That relation is not manufactured by a favorable outcome label. It remains an explicit substantive epistemic interpretation. [F3]

## Universality is not supplied by unrestricted self-reference

A system might examine its standards indefinitely while remaining confined to one kind of geometry puzzle. Another might have a language capable of naming every scientific discipline but no organization capable of learning or creating explanations in them. K-UNIVERSALITY excludes these cases by requiring explanatory capacity, not merely reflective vocabulary. Its subject-matter domain and enabling conditions must be specified independently of whatever the candidate happens to handle. [B6; B7]

## Physical realization is not supplied by abstract consistency

A coherent description of a capacity need not be physically realizable. Conversely, reliable physical transformation does not by itself establish semantic authorship. K-PHYSICALITY supplies a separate realization question and prevents either inference. The physical vocabulary and its limits are retained without making implementation feasibility the organizing question of the semantic theory. [M5; M7]

# Part II. Semantic structure

## The model and the three standpoints

A model is an interpreted structure

\[
\mathcal M=\langle S,C,\mathcal H,\mathcal X,\mathrm{Rep},\mathrm{Prov},
\mathrm{CF},\mathrm{Account},\mathrm{Bearing},\mathrm{Progress},
\mathrm{Capacity},\Theta\rangle.
\]

Here \(S\) is a domain of systems and attributed contributors; \(C\) is a domain of contents; \(\mathcal H\) is a nonempty family of histories and admitted counterfactual histories; and \(\mathcal X\) is a domain of problem situations. The remaining entries interpret representation, provenance, counterfactual dependence, explanatory accounting, the bearing of reasons, improvement, and capacity. \(\Theta\) supplies physical assumptions when physical attribution is being made. It is not needed to turn every purely semantic comparison into a physical experiment.

This signature is an organizational vocabulary, not a derivation of its own interpretations. In particular, \(\mathrm{Account}\), \(\mathrm{Bearing}\), \(\mathrm{Progress}\), and \(\mathrm{Capacity}\) carry substantive content. Their admissibility conditions are stated below. Calling one of them true is not a substitute for explaining why it holds in an application.

Three standpoints must remain distinct. **Model satisfaction**, written \(\mathcal M,\xi\vDash\phi\), states what holds in a specified interpretation at situation \(\xi\). **Situated judgment**, written \(\mathrm{Judges}(s,\phi,\xi)\), states what the system takes to hold. **Attribution evidence** is the material an investigator uses to conjecture that the model and the situated judgment appropriately describe a target. None of these is identified with either of the others.

An occurrence of a claim does not assert that claim as a premise of the metatheory. A model can therefore describe an agent entertaining inconsistent proposals without itself asserting both proposals as true.

Ordinary mathematical consequence is available to the author of a theorem about the model. It is not an instruction that the modeled thinker must execute a formal calculus, and it does not eliminate the fallibility of selecting a model or interpreting its application.

## Contents, occurrences, and interpretations

A content can play the role of a problem, conjecture, explanation, observation claim, criticism, response, standard, method, inference, interpretation, value, or attribution. These roles can overlap. A criticism is a conjecture about a defect; an observation claim contains assumptions; an account of a standard may itself be an explanation.

A content occurrence is a particular token, artifact, event-associated representation, or embodied state. Its identity is distinct from its interpreted content. Several occurrences may express the same content. One occurrence may support several defensible interpretations, and an interpreter may be mistaken about which content it carries.

An interpretation is indexed by an explicit semantic grain \(\ell\), a context, and an attributed system. A relation \(x\equiv_\ell y\) may be used where genuine equivalence at that grain is intended. Similarity is not silently promoted to equivalence: a tolerance relation that is not transitive remains a similarity relation. Changing a notation can preserve explanatory organization; identical wording can conceal a changed problem, scope, or inferential role.

An explicit sentence, a diagram, an action, and an inexplicit disposition can all carry content. No complete formal translation is required. The interpretation must nevertheless explain systematic use, discrimination, reconstruction, or correction by the system. An analyst’s ability to draw a suggestive graph is not sufficient. Nor does the inability to verbalize a discrimination establish that no content is present. [B14; B16]

A formal representation is a conjectural interpretation of the relevant content. It does not erase the prose, artifact, or inexplicit role it represents. Where the formal representation loses a distinction on which the explanation depends, the formal representation is inadequate in that respect.

## Histories and causal order

Each history \(h\in\mathcal H\) contains occurrences with a causal precedence relation \(\prec_h\). A reference to an actual earlier occurrence requires that occurrence to exist in the relevant history. A prediction or conjecture about a future event instead refers to proposed content, not to an already accomplished historical occurrence.

Actual temporal location and explanatory dependence must not be conflated. Earlier knowledge can be relevant to novelty even when the event in which it was acquired is not listed as a direct premise of a later conjecture. Likewise, an explanation of a structure is not necessarily an explanation of a later event by an earlier event. [F1]

A historical cut is a portion of a history selected for a question. It need not be the end of the system’s activity. A finite archive can describe an unbounded history, and a short argument can concern an infinite class. Neither histories nor the semantic domains are required to be finite, finitely enumerable, or completely represented in an observer’s records.

Possibilities that cannot jointly occur in one history can be represented in different histories. By contrast, incompatible conjectures can coexist as contents of one actual inquiry. Logical incompatibility between propositions is not physical impossibility of entertaining both propositions.

## Problem situations and episodes

A problem situation is represented schematically as

\[
\xi=\langle P,B,V,K,A,J,L\rangle.
\]

\(P\) contains the live problems and their current formulations; \(B\) the background contents in use; \(V\) the values and priorities affecting attention; \(K\) the standards, methods, and interpretation practices in use; \(A\) the actual alternatives under consideration; \(J\) situated judgments; and \(L\) links to relevant histories, dependencies, and retained resources. These components describe semantic roles, not necessarily explicit lists inside a thinker.

A situation index identifies the respects relevant to the claim, including the problem formulation, background, standards, values where material, and interpretation. Abbreviating a situation to a problem–background pair does not assert that omitted components are unchanged or irrelevant. A comparison that changes a material component must expose that change.

A represented problem is a difficulty experienced or recognized by the attributed system against that background. A problem imposed by another person can become the system’s own live problem through understanding. Problem-origin autonomy, candidate-origin autonomy, criticism-origin autonomy, and autonomy in changing standards are separate attributions.

An episode \(q\) is a connected course of attention to a difficulty, including its reformulations and related subproblems. The relevant connectivity is causal and explanatory, not mere topic similarity. Episodes can overlap, split, resume, or be absorbed into another inquiry. An earlier contribution may be imported into a later episode without pretending that its historical occurrence took place there.

An episode’s engagement state and its epistemic disposition are separate. Work can be active, suspended, interrupted, or closed for now. A system can move on after accepting, rejecting, or setting aside a proposal. Closing the episode does not make its conclusion immune. A later episode can reopen the issue. An observer’s decision to stop recording is not, by itself, a decision by the system.

## The recursive kernel as a relation, not a scheduler

Write

\[
\mathcal K(s;q;\xi,\xi')
\]

for a connected instance of problem-directed conjecture and critical activity by \(s\), relating situations \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\). The relation does not prescribe a fixed number of stages, an order of agenda items, a final winner, or termination. Some occurrences are incomplete engagements; not every interval supplies a complete critical episode. The actuality of a critical engagement does not entail the correctness of its objection or its interpretation of the reason.

A complete critical episode includes a represented difficulty, a target content already available for criticism, a conjectural objection represented as bearing on that difficulty, and a reason-sensitive response that leaves a resulting problem situation. That situation can contain unresolved questions or qualified retention rather than a new candidate theory. A creative critical episode additionally contains an attributed originative explanatory contribution connected to the engagement.

The recursive commitment has three parts.

**Target closure.** Any content, method, interpretation, or relation doing work in an instance of \(\mathcal K\) may become the represented subject of another instance. A system need not possess an exhaustive self-description to question “the rule by which I preferred this result.” That description can itself be improved.

**Role preservation.** Scrutiny of a method uses the same distinctions between conjecture, reason-bearing criticism, response, and improvement. There is no special kind of criticism whose correctness follows from its higher position. A proposed criticism of criticism remains a conjecture with a target and a bearing claim.

**Return relevance.** A result of the subsidiary inquiry can revise the parent inquiry’s use of the target. It can withdraw a premise, alter an interpretation, change which comparison is appropriate, narrow a conclusion, or redirect attention. It need not change the parent’s conclusion when the objection fails, and mere chronological succession does not establish that it had any effect.

These are conditions on semantic organization. They do not assert that every imaginable criticism is immediately available, that an actual person can formulate every needed objection, or that every potential subproblem must be pursued. A realized capacity claim adds the appropriate modal obligations. An actual failure of recognition remains a possible failure, not a contradiction manufactured by the definition of fallibility.

## Methods can be criticized from within inquiry

A system’s operative method at \(\xi\) is represented by contents in \(K\), together with their actual roles. It can conjecture a description of that method, criticize the description or the method, and introduce a different practice at \(\xi'\). The account of the transition belongs to the same history as the inquiry it changes.

This is not a requirement for a mechanism to rewrite a currently executing evaluator. It is a requirement that method change not be intelligible only as an unexplained intervention by an external authority. A mathematician changing a proof strategy, an investigator rejecting an instrument model, and a community revising an evidential convention can be instances of it.

The mathematical language used to describe this theory is not thereby made into a perfect internal truth predicate for a thinker. A representation of “my present method is adequate” is a conjecture in the model; it is not identical to the metatheoretic assertion that it is adequate. The theory itself remains open to criticism and replacement, but a theorem must keep its meanings fixed while its consequence is being claimed.

## Chronology does not decide opposition between reasons

Causal order belongs to occurrences. Explanatory dependence, support, opposition, and undercutting belong to interpreted contents and applications. These relations need not inherit the acyclicity of causal order.

Suppose two arguments are already available. A later inquiry can recognize that each conflicts with a premise used by the other. Both relations can be recorded after both arguments exist. There is no backward causation. Similarly, two methods can challenge the standards by which each claims precedence. Such a situation may remain unresolved until a new distinction or argument is created.

Acyclicity is therefore not imposed on semantic opposition. The most recently stated unanswered objection is not privileged merely by its date. Circular opposition does not imply that all participants are equally good, and circular support does not create independent warrant by circulating it. Those are explanatory questions about the content and dependencies of the reasons.

## Attempting an explanation and actually explaining

An attempted explanation is content the system deploys as an account of a represented difficulty. It can be false, incomplete, poorly articulated, or based on a mistaken problem. It is not required to have answered every objection before it counts as an attempt.

A useful interpretive description of such content is

\[
E=\langle W,\Gamma,\Delta,\Sigma,B_E\rangle,
\]

where \(W\) describes its explanatory job, \(\Gamma\) its commitments, \(\Delta\) the proposed explanatory connections, \(\Sigma\) its scope, and \(B_E\) its auxiliaries. This is a way of exposing what the proposal does, not a mandatory artifact or a complete analysis of explanation.

The substantive relation

\[
\mathrm{Account}_{\mathcal M}(e,p,b;\Sigma)
\]

holds when the interpreted content accounts for the relevant feature of the problem in the stated respect and scope. An admissible interpretation must identify the explanatory work done by the commitments, explain the bearing of the connections, and distinguish that work from merely associating an input with an output. A route through a diagram is not enough unless the links on that route do the claimed work.

An account may invoke a mechanism, geometry, a constraint, an abstract structure, an intention, an institution, or another appropriate explanatory mode. No single microscopic causal vocabulary is imposed on every domain. A false attempted mechanism is still an explanatory conjecture; its conjectural role must not be confused with successful accounting for reality. [F1; B1]

An interpreter need not establish that every commitment is consciously used at one instant. Use may be distributed across an episode, embodied in a disposition, or shared by a group. Several partial accounts can contribute to one explanation. Their integration requires an account of how the parts work together, not a requirement that they were born in a single complete graph.

## Reason-bearing criticism and counterfactual uptake

A criticism is interpretable as

\[
c=\langle z,p,b,\delta,g,\lambda\rangle,
\]

where \(z\) is the target, \(\delta\) the alleged defect, \(g\) the grounds or reasons, and \(\lambda\) the proposed bearing of those grounds on the target in problem \(p\). A standard used by the criticism belongs among its dependencies. A test, discriminator, or proposed investigation may be part of the grounds; an empirical experiment is not mandatory for every criticism.

\(\mathrm{Bearing}_{\mathcal M}(g,\delta,z,p,b)\) is a substantive relation. A criticism occurrence can allege that relation without it holding. This permits bad criticisms, misunderstood objections, and mistaken rejections to be represented without treating them as valid defeaters.

For a response \(\alpha\),

\[
\mathrm{UsesReason}_{\mathcal M}(s,c,\alpha;\mathfrak C)
\]

requires an explanatory account of how the represented reason participates in the response across a relevant contrast family \(\mathfrak C\). Content-preserving recodings should preserve the relevant form of uptake, where the recodings are understood. Changes to the alleged defect should make a content-appropriate difference to deliberation, investigation, scope, assessment of a local commitment, or response. Irrelevant hostility should not be counted as the same criticism simply because it causes an output change.

These contrasts concern relevant semantic effects, not necessarily different final words. Two different objections can rationally receive the same action, such as suspension, for different recognized reasons. A system can also be misled while genuinely using a reason. Perfect discrimination of every invalid objection is not a requirement for every instance of uptake.

An empty or semantically uninformative contrast family supplies no discrimination between reason use and generic responsiveness. The relevant alternatives and held-fixed conditions must be appropriate to the actual content and organization; they cannot be selected solely to obtain a favorable attribution.

Actually performed interventions can be evidence for this relation, but a fixed battery of interventions is not part of its identity. The relation can be assessed through a broader causal explanation of the organization. Where the evidence is inadequate, attribution remains unresolved; the semantic phenomenon is not defined out of existence. [B7]

## Argument dependencies and the effect of criticizing a premise

An argument occurrence \(u\) has a conclusion, a scope, an inferential application, and the premises on which that application essentially depends. A source cited for historical background is not necessarily an essential premise. Conversely, a standard, interpretation, observation model, or comparison can be essential even when it was not packaged as an explicit certificate.

Essentiality is application-relative:

\[
\mathrm{Essential}(d,u)
\]

means that this particular argument application cannot sustain its stated conclusion in its stated way without \(d\). It does not mean that no different argument could support the same conclusion. Several independent arguments can have the same conclusion.

For a situated appraisal \(j\), let \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d)\) mean that \(d\) is being treated as an available premise in the specified respect. Define

\[
\mathrm{Usable}_j(u)\iff
\mathrm{Licensed}_j(u)\land\mathrm{InScope}_j(u)
\land \forall d\,[\mathrm{Essential}(d,u)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Standing}_j(d)].
\]

Here \(\mathrm{Licensed}_j\) concerns the inferential application, not an infallible authorization. Its basis is itself criticizable. The displayed condition expresses an obligation of a coherent appraisal: one cannot both withdraw an essential premise in the relevant respect and continue to claim support from the very same dependent application as though nothing changed.

This applies uniformly to a theory, a criticism, a criticism of a criticism, a standard’s application, or a claim of progress. There is no exemption designed to preserve a convenient shape of argument graph. A system can violate this obligation; the semantics then represents an error in its appraisal rather than silently declaring the violation rational.

Standing is indexed by the use and respect at issue. Where a single symbol would conceal different uses, write \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d;u)\). A premise can be available for one qualified application and unavailable for another. Ordinary conjectures, observations, comparisons, reasons, background assumptions, and standards can all occupy this role. A representation that admits only certificates and standards as dependency targets is not a representation of the general obligation. Provisional background assumptions are allowed; they need not be conclusions of completed prior proofs. Their provisional use and their vulnerability to relevant criticism must both be represented.

The obligation concerns coherent appraisal, not an automatic instruction that any newly entered objection defeats its target. Whether an alleged defect bears on the premise, whether its grounds are acceptable in that appraisal, and which applications depend on the premise are themselves criticizable judgments. A schematic attack edge or a record of disagreement cannot supply those interpretations by itself.


A defeated argument does not make its conclusion false. A withdrawn criticism does not make the criticized claim true. A different usable argument may still sustain the conclusion. A criticism of only one application of a standard need not defeat every use of that standard: the defect and scope determine which dependencies are affected.

A support cycle supplies no new independent reason merely because it is a cycle. Nevertheless, mutually constraining theories, provisional background assumptions, simultaneous equations, and reciprocal explanatory relations are not prohibited. Their adequacy depends on their content and connections to independently relevant constraints, not on whether a graph algorithm can label every node.

## Appraisal is also conjectural content

An appraisal can propose that an explanation is preferable, a criticism succeeds, a standard is defective, a question is ill-posed, or a comparison is currently unresolved. Those proposals are contents eligible for the same kernel. The appraisal’s method, interpretation, and substantive premises must be recoverable in the explanatory account, although no particular storage format is prescribed.

A record may distinguish “no case supplied,” “a positive case supplied,” “a negative case supplied,” and “cases supplied on both sides.” These are optional descriptions of the record. They are not four kinds of truth, and the mere presence or absence of an unanswered attack does not determine explanatory merit.

In particular, a well-formed account of a failed test is not evidence that the test succeeded. Keeping a defective argument available for examination is different from counting it as a usable argument. Conversely, assessing an argument as unusable does not require erasing it or making the rejection immune to scrutiny.

No complete or unique ranking of all alternatives is assumed. Incomparability, disagreement about criteria, and unfinished assessment are permitted. Moving from them to a reasoned provisional preference may require creating a new explanation, not merely selecting a different rule for aggregating the same old labels.

## Newness is relative to an attributed system and a semantic grain

Let \(R^{\ell,\beta}_{<e}(s,h)\) contain the explanatory contents that the system, under attribution boundary \(\beta\) and its declared historical continuity, had previously been able to deploy in a relevant explanatory role before event \(e\). Initial understanding belongs to this baseline. Previously possessed but subsequently forgotten understanding also belongs when the claim is first creation by that continuing system.

Mere storage of a string is not automatically understanding. Nor are all logical consequences of an accepted theory automatically in the system’s possessed repertoire. Discovering how a known theory accounts for a new phenomenon may require new explanatory work. [F1]

Define

\[
\mathrm{New}_{\ell,\beta}(s,x,e,h)
\iff
\neg\exists y\in R^{\ell,\beta}_{<e}(s,h)\;(y\equiv_\ell x).
\]

Historical novelty replaces the system-relative baseline by an independently specified historical one. Present reacquisition and first creation by the continuing system are different questions. Neither baseline is silently switched to make an attribution come out favorably.

The relation concerns the actual interpreted repertoire, not just an investigator’s archive. An incomplete archive can leave newness unestablished. A complete inventory of the entries in that archive does not by itself establish a complete inventory of the system’s past understanding.

“Previously able to deploy” means that the system actually possessed the relevant explanatory ability at an earlier time, not that it could have acquired it after unspecified additional learning. Causal ancestry of a particular output is not an inventory of every earlier possession. An earlier understanding can defeat a first-creation claim even when no explicit dependency edge connects it to the later output. A completeness claim about the actual repertoire therefore needs an account relating the evidence to that repertoire, not just an exhaustive scan of recorded tokens.


## Authorship and distributed contribution

Write

\[
\mathrm{Authors}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e)
\]

when the causal-explanatory account attributes the construction or reconstruction of the relevant problem-specific organization in \(x\), in the contribution completed or delimited at \(e\) in \(h\), to \(s\). This is not shorthand for “every incoming channel lacked equivalent content.” It requires an account of the contribution.

The contribution index is essential. A system may first forward an explanation without understanding it and later reconstruct it. Its later authorship does not make the earlier forwarding an originative act. All conjuncts of OCA concern the same contribution, content interpretation, problem, history, boundary, and grain. The index can delimit an extended or distributed activity; it does not require the construction to occur in a single instantaneous event. An occurrence is not identified with its date of entry in an investigator’s archive.

A received explanation may be understood only after the learner invents an interpretation, tests it against the problem, and corrects it. The sender and learner can then receive different attributions: the sender for the earlier explanation, the learner for its reconstruction. A device that forwards the same text without such a role receives transmission credit instead. Prior influence is compatible with authorship; mere causal involvement is not sufficient for it. [B16]

The boundary \(\beta\) identifies the system whose contribution is claimed. Nested attributions to a person, a collaboration, and an institution are permissible, provided each has its own causal account. A later discovery that a tool or collaborator did essential explanatory work can revise the attribution. This does not move a past event; it corrects the account of who contributed what.

Every use of the authorship and newness predicates displays \(s,\beta,\ell\), the relevant history, and the contribution index. A definition must not existentially search for a favorable boundary while presenting its result as an answer about a boundary fixed by the question.

## Originative acts and critical processes

An originative explanatory act is defined by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{OCA}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e)\iff{}&
\mathrm{Attempt}(s,x,p,h,e)\\
&\land\mathrm{New}_{\ell,\beta}(s,x,e,h)\\
&\land\mathrm{Authors}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e).
\end{aligned}
\]

\(\mathrm{Attempt}\) requires a represented problem and the use of \(x\) as an attempted account of it. No truth, success, later retention, critical episode, or universal capacity is a conjunct. A one-time false conjecture can be an originative act.

A **critical episode** requires connected, reason-bearing criticism and uptake, whether the original target was authored by the responder or inherited. A **creative critical episode** additionally contains an OCA that contributes to that connected inquiry. The contribution may be a candidate explanation, a reconstructed understanding, an explanation of a defect, or a new explanatory account of the problem or method.

The OCA need not originate the content first criticized in the episode. A system can inherit a theory, criticize it, and create an explanatory account of its defect or a replacement. The inherited target must precede criticism of that target; the new contribution need not. Connection is explanatory and causal: membership in an arbitrarily named episode is insufficient, but descent from one originally authored theory is not necessary. A lineage-specific revision process is a special case, not the definition of a creative critical episode.


A **critical result** is the resulting problem situation together with the contents and dispositions that the response makes available. It is not restricted to a single new theory token. Suspension can produce a better articulated uncertainty. Rejecting a criticism can reveal a misunderstanding. Requesting evidence can formulate a new discriminating question. None is guaranteed to create knowledge, but none is excluded merely by its outcome name.

A **reason-sensitive setting aside** is distinguished from a content-insensitive refusal. Either may occur in a history. An unexplained refusal must not be given a fictional reason in order to fit a schema. Where an actual reason is inexplicit, its attribution has the same causal and semantic burden as other inexplicit contents.

## Continuity through reframing, replacement, and absorption

Ancestry of content occurrences is one kind of provenance, not the whole of explanatory continuity. A theory can be replaced by an independently originated rival. An episode can preserve an earlier problem’s difficulty while changing its formulation, background, or standards. A result may join several lines of inquiry.

A contextual transport

\[
T:\langle p,b,k\rangle\rightsquigarrow\langle p',b',k'\rangle
\]

is an interpreted account of which difficulties, commitments, and reasons are preserved, revised, abandoned, or rendered inapplicable. Its validity is a substantive claim, not an automatic consequence of writing the arrow.

A connected critical history using \(T\) must show how the earlier difficulty bears on the later one and how the response contributed to the change. It must not continue evaluating all descendants against the original problem by fiat. Nor may it discard the original obligations merely by renaming the problem. The transport and any claim that it improved the situation remain criticizable.

Absorbing an earlier result into another episode changes the later episode’s resources and explanatory organization. The earlier event keeps its historical location. Reuse of history is not rewriting history.

## Explanatory quality, constraint, and reach

A current good explanation is an account that addresses the relevant problem and withstands the applicable criticism sufficiently well to merit preference among the actual alternatives in the stated scope. This is not a requirement that its present user originated it, and it does not make the user’s preference self-validating. An inherited explanation can be the best available account. [F3; B1]

A useful analysis of “hard to vary” asks which content-bearing details are constrained by the work the explanation does. An apparently harmless alteration is a substantive criticism when it shows that the alleged explanatory connection contributed nothing: the account can be changed without supplying a new reason and without losing the very explanatory work it claimed to do. [B1]

For a specified commitment \(d\), job \(p\), background \(b\), and relevant variation family \(\mathcal V\), write

\[
\mathrm{FreeVariation}_{\mathcal V}(e,d;p,b)
\]

when an explanatory-detail change in that family preserves the claimed account without a relevant new explanation or compensating explanatory change. The family must contain genuinely informative contrasts. An empty family, a renaming family, or a family constructed to exclude the objection does not establish nonarbitrariness.

This diagnostic is comparative and respect-relative. It is not a demand that every possible modification be disastrous. Two independently reasoned explanations can address the same problem; the existence of one is not automatically a free variation of the other. A false explanation can have tightly constrained internal details. Constraint is therefore neither uniqueness nor truth certification.

The relevant comparison concerns the explanatory work the proposal purports to do, not only the work of alternatives already known to be true. Otherwise a false myth with many equally arbitrary false variants could count as hard to vary merely because none of those variants actually explains reality. A finding of arbitrariness can concern a particular unconstrained detail without rendering every commitment worthless. Conversely, inspecting no material variations does not establish constraint.


Explanatory reach is a relation between the commitments of an explanation and further problems for which they do explanatory work. It can exist before any agent recognizes it. Its scope is not restricted to an enumerated collection of successful applications. [B1]

An application claim must identify the unchanged commitments, the additional background facts, and the explanation of their relevance. Supplying the answer in the alleged background is not an explanation of reach. A genuinely new bridge theory may extend usable understanding, but that bridge is an additional creative contribution and should not be concealed as a free consequence of the old theory.

## Progress, possession, and historical assessment

Let

\[
\mathrm{Progress}_{\mathcal M}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)
\]

mean that contribution \(\Delta\) produces an actual explanatory improvement in the stated problem situation. The interpretation must identify what deficiency has been removed or clarified, what useful explanatory work survives or is superseded, what losses matter, and why the new situation is better in the claimed respect. A bare assertion that a preferred standard was met is insufficient.

Examples include replacing a false mechanism, explaining why a previous approximation worked, discovering that an apparent conflict was caused by a faulty interpretation, finding a better question, or recognizing a limit that prevents a spurious answer. These are not equivalent successes, and the theory imposes no common numerical scale on them.

An improvement claim can be local. Local progress does not establish an improvement everywhere, and disclosing a loss does not make it acceptable. A more comprehensive superiority claim needs a correspondingly comprehensive comparison. Changed standards must be part of the explanation of the comparison, not its concealed premise.

**Explanatory knowledge creation** by a system consists of a connected creative critical contribution, actual explanatory progress attributable to it, and the resulting knowledge being available for use or reconstruction by the attributed system or explicitly identified community. Availability is not endorsement and does not require perpetual survival. Whether an insight is created, retained by its creator, communicated, or lost are distinct historical questions. [F3; M5]

A historical occurrence of “we judged this a correction” remains an occurrence even after that judgment is rejected. A claim that the correction really improved situation \(\xi\) relative to \(\xi'\) remains a truth claim about those indexed situations; discovering that the claim was wrong does not make it formerly true. Later usefulness in a new situation is another question. Keeping those indices explicit prevents changed evidence from masquerading as changed history.

## What explanatory universality requires

The domain of a universality claim must be specified independently of the candidate’s successes. For full explanatory universality, let \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) range over explanatory contents across every subject matter admitted by the adopted account of reality, subject to the physical possibility of their relevant representation under \(\Theta\). Let \(\mathfrak P_\Theta\) range over the corresponding coherently posed explanatory challenges. These classes include unfamiliar subjects and explanations not yet invented. They are not a test suite, a fixed dictionary inside the system, or the definition “whatever this system can already handle.”

A restriction to a specified subject \(D\) yields a domain-relative capacity, which must be reported with that restriction. Even universality within two large independently chosen subjects is not full explanatory universality. Merely choosing the domain independently of the candidate is necessary but insufficient: the full claim may not exclude a subject solely because the candidate lacks its concepts or presently cannot address it. The account of physical and explanatory admissibility, including the boundary of the domain itself, remains criticizable.

An explanatory challenge can concern why a requested transformation is impossible. It need not require that the requested transformation be achieved. A demand for the unrecoverable accidental details of an unrecorded event is not automatically equivalent to a demand for a general explanation. The distinction must be made on explanatory and physical grounds, not by removing inconvenient failures after the fact.

Let \(\chi\) specify enabling conditions: relevant background learning, evidence, time, memory, communication, tools, and other resources. \(\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,p,\chi)\) requires that these conditions be physically admitted where a physical claim is made, and that their specification not simply assert that the desired discovery has already occurred. They need not be identical for every problem.

Each challenge also specifies an **achievement respect** and the permitted contributions of its enabling environment. These are fixed by an explanatory account of the challenge, independently of the candidate’s success. Understanding a supplied explanation, reconstructing previously absent understanding, and originating a solution not supplied by the environment are different achievements. A condition suitable for one cannot silently discharge another. Full creative-inquiry scope includes challenges requiring an explanatory contribution not already supplied as the requested solution; it is not exhausted by the class of learning tasks with solved answers available. This achievement-sensitive interpretation of UC is an explicit modeling commitment, not a theorem derived merely from the ability to understand communicated explanations.

A source may communicate relevant background, a failed approach, an objection, or an explanation that the system subsequently extends. Such communication is compatible with creativity. The restriction concerns where the *claimed* organization was created, not whether a communication channel was used. When the challenge is reconstruction, supplying the explanation to be understood is legitimate. When the challenge is origination of an unsupplied solution, reconstructing a supplied complete solution is a different achievement and must be reported as such.


Let

\[
\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,p,\chi;\Phi)
\]

mean that the continuing organization \(\Omega\), at boundary \(\beta\), has the capacity to perform the explanatory activity \(\Phi\) on problem \(p\) under \(\chi\). This is a substantive modal relation, not bare existence of a lucky history. Its interpretation must explain how the organization contributes to the activity, how relevant variations in the problem or reasons affect it, and why the activity is not merely a preadapted answer supplied by an unacknowledged external creator.

A capacity may admit failed attempts and interruptions. It need not survive a perturbation that destroys the capacity and all means of restoring it. Robustness claims state which perturbations and recovery conditions they cover. Mere absence of a witnessed failure does not establish capacity; one failed attempt does not establish incapacity. An explanatory argument about an architectural barrier can refute a capacity attribution without exhaustively enumerating every possible continuation.

The same \(\Omega\) across domains means explanatory continuity of the learning and critical organization, not frozen contents or unalterable internal methods. It may acquire new languages, change standards, build tools, and reorganize. Replacing it with an unrelated already competent system does not establish that the original organization had the capacity. Shared achievements are attributed to the declared composite where appropriate.

The attribution boundary and continuity criterion are held fixed across the universal quantifiers. An account may explain how the organization recruits tools or collaborators; it may not choose a new competent author for each problem and call the resulting assortment the same system. A family of enabling conditions must expose its common resource and contribution discipline. Merely relabeling an answer-bearing device as “background learning,” or postulating a capacity predicate separately for every desired conclusion, does not meet that burden. This is an explanatory constraint, not a requirement for a computable uniform resource-selection rule.


### Universal understanding

A universal understanding capacity has no fixed explanatory domain bar:

\[
\mathrm{UU}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)
\iff
\forall e\in\mathfrak E_\Theta\;\exists\chi\;
\bigl(\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,\mathrm{understand}(e),\chi)
\land\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,\mathrm{understand}(e),\chi;
\mathrm{UnderstandAndCriticize})\bigr).
\]

Here \(\mathrm{understand}(e)\) is the challenge of reconstructing the relevant explanatory organization, not merely producing its words. Communication of \(e\) is allowed: understanding communicated explanations is part of the capacity. It does not confer historical invention credit. The quantifier does not claim that all explanations can be understood simultaneously or within one fixed lifetime or resource bound.

### Universal creative inquiry

A universal creative-inquiry capacity must be able to form and critically pursue problems across \(\mathfrak P_\Theta\), including problems about its own methods. It cannot satisfy the requirement merely by issuing empty conjectures about every named topic.

For challenges for which explanatory improvement is possible in principle, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{UC}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff
\forall p\in\mathfrak P_\Theta^{\mathrm{adv}}\;\exists\chi\;&
\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,p,\chi)\\
{}\land{}&\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}
(s,p,\chi;\mathrm{CreativeCriticalAdvance}).
\end{aligned}
\]

\(\mathfrak P_\Theta^{\mathrm{adv}}\) is selected by the independent explanatory claim that a suitable advance is possible, not by a claim about \(s\). Its interpretation must say what counts as an advance, including correction of false presuppositions where appropriate. A vacuous empty domain is not admitted, and genuinely different subject matters cannot be collapsed by relabeling them as one solved problem.

For this clause, the causal organization of the advance must be attributable to \(s\) or the declared composite. Resources can supply prior knowledge and relevant evidence, but a successful performance consisting only of having the complete answer provided and copying it does not witness creative advance. A communicated result can instead occasion a genuine new reconstruction or a further explanatory contribution; that contribution must be identified rather than assumed.

The strength of UC is intentional. The absence of an explicit prohibition is weaker than a capacity to advance. An account that establishes only permission to inquire, or only topic-independent syntax, must report that weaker result rather than claiming UC.

### Universal explanatory disposition

The target class is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{UED}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff{}&
\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}_{\beta}(s,\Omega)\\
&\land\mathrm{UU}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\\
&\land\mathrm{UC}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega).
\end{aligned}
\]

\(\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}\) requires the target closure, role preservation, and return relevance of the kernel as capacities of the same continuing organization, under appropriate enabling conditions. They are not supplied solely by a separate observer who can write comments after the target has ceased functioning.

The **base semantic class** consists of interpretations respecting the distinctions and structural commitments stated here. It can describe mistaken judgments, failed critical episodes, and institutions that obstruct criticism without declaring those failures to be rational. The **recursive-capacity class** additionally satisfies the kernel’s three modal conditions for an attributed organization. The **universal explanatory class** additionally satisfies UU and UC over the unrestricted explanatory domain. Nonuniversal systems remain comparison cases; an isolated OCA need not belong to either stronger class.

The three-conjunct decomposition of UED is a modeling proposal intended to make the universal claim substantial and contestable; it is not a theorem that every instance of creativity already has these capacities. Understanding, progress, and continuing capacity have substantive interpretations, so the displayed quantifiers do not by themselves explain their realization.

Understanding and creative advance are not inferred from a system’s merely being able to name a problem. An explanation of universal capacity must connect its method of acquiring representations to its method of criticizing and extending them. Otherwise a domain barrier can have been hidden in the supposedly neutral representational interface.

A theory of a universal organization should explain why its way of creating, reconstructing, and criticizing explanatory content is not tied to a finite preadapted repertoire. That account can support a fallible universal attribution. The finite record alone is not a deductive guarantee, but neither is the lack of such a guarantee a prohibition on explanatory knowledge of universality. [B6; B7]

## Physical realization and constructor-theoretic scope

For physical attribution, an interpretation must connect content roles to physically discriminable states, the claimed activities to physically admitted processes, and counterfactual differences in reasons to corresponding differences in the organization’s use of them. The same interpretation must be used throughout the comparisons. It cannot be changed separately for each result to manufacture conformity.

The boundary includes the stores, collaborators, tools, and environmental contributions that matter to the claim. Distributed realization is allowed; a list of contributors without an account of their integration is not enough. Resources and error correction matter where repeatable capacity is claimed, but the ability to continue indefinitely is not inserted into the definition of a one-time originative act.

For a physical realization module, the words *substrate*, *attribute*, and *task* denote respectively a physical system, a relevant set of its states, and specified input-to-output transformations. These are adopted modeling definitions, not additional explanatory premises. The important physical distinction is between a transformation occurring once and an organization retaining the ability to cause it. Marletto develops that distinction through generalized and abstract catalysts; her counterfactual account concerns what physical laws permit, not simply which apparatus someone presently possesses. These physical notions are not definitions of explanatory understanding. [M5; M7]

Physical knowledge in Marletto’s account is information with a causal capacity for preserving its instantiation in suitable circumstances. An inherited biological adaptation can have that character without representing an explanation. Conversely, the epistemic merit of an explanatory discovery is not identical to the duration for which one copy survives. The exact persistence environment and horizon belong to the physical claim. [M5]

Universality of construction and universality of explanation are therefore separate attributions. Inferring construction capacity requires an account of how know-how, suitable physical resources, and machinery can realize the relevant tasks. It is not obtained just by unfolding UED. No claim about consciousness, personhood, moral standing, or species membership follows from a lone episode or from a bare constructor description.

# Part III. Consequences and discriminating examples

## Historical acyclicity does not imply semantic acyclicity

**Claim.** An inquiry with no backward causal reference can contain mutual semantic opposition.

**Construction.** Let \(A\) and \(B\) be two already represented arguments, one concluding proposition \(p\) and the other concluding \(\neg p\), with their respective reasons. At later occurrences \(u\) and \(v\), the inquirer recognizes and records the two directed rebuttal relations between their incompatible conclusions. Both occurrences refer only to existing contents. The historical precedence relation remains acyclic. The opposition relation on the two arguments nevertheless contains \(A\to B\) and \(B\to A\).

**Consequence.** Removing mutual opposition to preserve a chronological graph loses a legitimate explanatory situation. It does not discover a universal absence of circular disagreement. This result depends only on the distinction between occurrences and contents and on the permitted interpretation of opposition.

## Losing a premise loses an argument, not automatically its conclusion

**Claim.** If an essential premise of argument \(u\) loses standing in appraisal \(j\), then \(u\) is not usable in that appraisal. The conclusion need not be false or unsupported by every other argument.

**Derivation.** The universal premise clause in the definition of \(\mathrm{Usable}_j\) fails. Now let another argument \(v\) have the same conclusion but no dependence on the withdrawn premise. If its licensing, scope, and own premises retain standing, its usability is unaffected. Nothing in either statement establishes the negation of the conclusion.

This consequence applies equally when \(u\) is a criticism. Exempting criticisms would violate the stated dependency condition, not merely choose a different reporting convention.

## Recursion is not universality

**Counterexample.** Consider an organization that can formulate conjectures about one limited subject, criticize those conjectures, and criticize its own standards within that subject. Its recursive inquiries may have arbitrarily many levels. Suppose its organization nevertheless prevents representation or critical learning of an independently specified explanatory problem \(p^*\) outside that subject under every admitted enabling condition.

The recursive condition can hold while the universal quantifier in UU or UC fails at \(p^*\). Thus K-RECURSION does not entail UED. This is why universality is a separate core commitment rather than an adjective attached to repetition.

## Provisional reasoning does not require a completed hierarchy of judges

**Construction.** An investigator uses a provisional instrument model to interpret a reading. A criticism identifies a particular vulnerability in that model. The investigator compares a revised model using other currently available explanations, while explicitly retaining unresolved assumptions. The resulting comparison changes the use of the original instrument model.

There is a finite explanatory episode with a genuine return from method criticism to object-level inquiry. None of its premises has been justified for all purposes, and each remains eligible for later criticism. The construction shows that recursive scrutiny need not require completed scrutiny of every background assumption before any local result is possible. It does not guarantee that every difficult recursion can be resolved.

## A finite record does not logically determine universal capacity

**Claim.** A finite record of successful episodes, without further organizational premises, does not entail UED.

**Construction.** Consider two candidate models that agree on the observed episodes, including whatever genuine creative contributions are established in them. Let one admit an organization satisfying the universal capacity clauses. Let the other have an unobserved domain barrier at a problem not covered by the record, while preserving the established past episodes. Both fit those observations. The second fails the relevant universal clause.

The construction is relative to a model class permitting both continuations; it is not a claim that two fully specified causal organizations can differ only by an arbitrary creativity label. Additional explanations of the organization can discriminate the candidates. The conclusion is underdetermination by the finite record alone, not a ban on rationally accepting a universal explanation.

## Explanation, creativity, and success do not collapse

A person can invent a false account: OCA holds but successful explanatory progress need not. A critic can respond to a real issue and introduce a worse theory: critical uptake holds but progress need not. A copier can transmit a correct explanation without creating its organization: transmission and possession of useful material do not establish authorship. A learner can reconstruct an old explanation: system-relative newness and reconstructive authorship can hold without historical firstness.

These are separations permitted by the stated definitions and substantive interpretations. None claims that copying, deduction, search, randomness, or communication is forbidden inside a creator. The relevant question is what explanatory work the organization performs.

## A worked inquiry: two balances and a changing question

This is a stipulated thought experiment, not a report of a real laboratory. Its simple mathematics makes the dependency problem visible without supplying a procedure for scientific discovery.

An investigator puts the same object on two balances. Balance A displays 10 units; balance B displays 12. She initially trusts B and proposes that A reads two units low. Her proposed account is not just the number 12: it is an explanation of the discrepancy in terms of one correct balance and one offset balance.

A colleague asks why B is trusted. If the answer is only that its reading agrees with the proposed mass, the account is using the conclusion to select its own standard. That objection targets the method of choosing a reference, not merely the measured number. The original problem, “What is wrong with A?”, becomes “What licenses treating either balance as the reference?”

For the moment, suppose each balance has a constant additive offset:

\[
m_A=x+b_A,\qquad m_B=x+b_B.
\]

The quantities \(m_A\) and \(m_B\) are the readings, \(x\) the object’s mass in the chosen unit, and \(b_A,b_B\) the proposed instrument offsets. The observations give

\[
10=x+b_A,\qquad12=x+b_B.
\]

The investigator’s first account is \((x,b_A,b_B)=(12,-2,0)\). A rival is \((10,0,2)\). The same observations fit both.

Now she notices a stronger point. For any admitted shift \(\lambda\), replacing

\[
(x,b_A,b_B)\quad\text{by}\quad
(x+\lambda,b_A-\lambda,b_B-\lambda)
\]

leaves both predicted readings unchanged. Within this additive model, the readings determine the offset difference \(b_B-b_A=2\), not the absolute value of \(x\). Repeating the same two readings does not remove that explanatory ambiguity. An additional independently relevant constraint is needed.

The useful result is not a newly certified mass. It is a better problem situation: the investigator now understands why her existing observations cannot answer the original question under the model she is using. She can seek calibration information, investigate the two instruments’ construction, or formulate another discriminating experiment. Suspending the numerical answer is compatible with making progress on the explanatory question.

The kernel has recurred. An instrument judgment became a target; criticism exposed a dependency; a conjecture about the measurement structure yielded a reasoned limitation; that result changed the original inquiry. No extra species of infallible metajudge was introduced.

The subsidiary result is also criticizable. Perhaps the offsets are not constant. Perhaps the balances respond differently to temperature or load. Those possibilities do not refute the algebra, but they can undermine applying that algebra to the apparatus. A new inquiry would then distinguish the validity of the mathematical consequence from the adequacy of the measurement model.

Suppose a calibration certificate is later supplied. Its existence does not close the issue. The certificate is evidence only through an account of what was calibrated, under which conditions, and why that calibration applies. A criticism of that applicability affects the argument using it, whether that argument supports a mass estimate or criticizes another estimate. The dependency rule does not change according to which side the argument serves.

Finally, suppose a collaborator discovers the invariance while the investigator merely repeats the result. The collaboration may have made an explanatory advance, but individual attribution requires a different account from the one in which the investigator herself understood and reconstructed the invariance. The mathematics is the same; the provenance is not.

## Explanatory constraint and reach without a list of outputs

Deutsch’s seasons example supplies a complementary case. Axial orientation, orbital relations, and the geometry of illumination constrain the account. Substituting an arbitrary agent’s changing mood does not preserve the explanatory work of those commitments. The explanation applies beyond the locations initially considered because of what it says, not because its author listed all successful geographical applications. [B1]

Its reach must still be stated accurately. A claim about seasonal illumination is not, without further explanation, a complete account of every local temperature maximum. Discovering the need for a further explanation is not a reason to conceal the distinction in a broader adequacy label. It can be the next problem generated by the successful account.

## Inexplicit and artistic inquiry

An artist can recognize that a passage does not achieve an intended effect, vary it, and distinguish a genuine improvement from an irrelevant alteration without possessing an explicit verbal theory of the difference. The semantic burden is to explain that problem-bearing discrimination and its use, not to require a preexisting formal specification of the finished work. [B14]

A theorist must not invent hidden standards solely to protect a desired classification. An attribution of inexplicit criticism needs a defensible account of the person’s discriminations, purposes, alternatives, and corrections. A claim that the work discovers objective aesthetic knowledge requires the further aesthetic interpretation; it does not follow merely from novelty, popularity, or the presence of a critical process. Deutsch’s account motivates that extension, while leaving substantial aesthetic questions open. [B14]

## Distributed inquiry and biological contrast

In a collaboration, one person may identify the problem, another invent a candidate, a third expose a defect in the measurement practice, and the group preserve a revised explanation. A system-level attribution requires the causal connections and shared use that make those contributions one inquiry. Agreement or common membership alone is not such a connection.

A biological lineage can acquire an adaptation through heritable variation and differential reproduction without representing the environmental difficulty as a problem or proposing a reason against a variant. It can instantiate physical knowledge without explanatory criticism. The shared variation-and-selection pattern does not eliminate this difference. Conversely, blind lower-level variation does not by itself eliminate the explanatory significance of a person’s higher-level thought. [F3; M5]

# Part IV. Adequacy, scope, and remaining questions

## What an application must explain

A proposed application must identify the system and attribution boundary, the represented difficulty, the relevant explanatory content, and the causal organization of the contribution. Where criticism is claimed, it must explain the reason’s bearing and uptake. Where progress is claimed, it must explain the improvement rather than substituting retention or a preferred verdict. Where recursive capacity is claimed, it must show how scrutiny can affect the operative use of methods and standards. Where universality is claimed, it must address the domain, enabling conditions, organizational continuity, and capacity clauses directly.

No complete transcript of a mind is required. No prescribed certificate format is required. No absence in an incomplete archive settles a claim about everything the system could know or do. The explanatory account can be incomplete, mistaken, or insufficiently discriminating; those are reasons to qualify the attribution, not to identify attribution with a document’s presence.

## What would count against the theory

The explanation-centered core would be challenged by a defensible account of explanatory knowledge creation that had no problem-directed conjectural or critical organization at the relevant level. The reason-use analysis would need revision if it systematically excluded genuine understanding or included content-insensitive compliance after the relevant causal differences were made explicit.

The recursive account would be inadequate if it allowed an operative standard to remain immune while claiming that criticism of it had substantive effect, or if it required a final justified evaluator to make any local progress. The contextual-transport account would fail if it either disconnected legitimate explanatory revolutions or permitted arbitrary problem changes to count as solutions.

The universality conditions would need revision if their resource clauses covertly supplied every target achievement, if their domains were selected by the candidate’s successes, or if the distinction between understanding and creating made them misdescribe universal explanatory ability. An attribution of UED would be challenged by a genuine organizational barrier to an independently characterized explanatory domain, under enabling conditions that do not simply remove the barrier by replacing the system.

These are substantive criticism routes. Renaming an inconvenient case, changing the scope silently, or declaring every negative case “not a real problem” would not answer them.

## What is and is not established

The theory supplies a recursive organization of semantic roles, an explicit universal target class, and distinctions that prevent several false identifications: history with judgment, criticism with rejection, understanding with transmission, explanatory progress with persistence, recursion with universality, and physical task capacity with creativity.

It does not derive the existence of an actual universal explainer from definitions. It does not give a complete reductive account of explanation, meaning, causal authorship, epistemic progress, or creative capacity. The source books motivate and constrain those relations without uniquely determining their full theory. The constructions here make additional commitments explicit and expose their consequences; their success must be assessed by the explanations they support and the criticisms they survive.

That limitation is not a requirement to turn the kernel into an algorithm. The remaining question is explanatory: what organization makes the attributed capacities possible, how do its parts do the claimed work, and why do the relevant distinctions hold? The same kernel applies to attempts to answer it.


## Refinement without semantic substitution

The following are proposed adequacy conditions for specifications adopting this theory. They make the authority relation explicit without requiring an implementation to decide the semantic predicates or treating these conditions as quotations from the source books.

A specification adopting this theory distinguishes a **semantic assertion**, an **attribution judgment**, a **record fact**, and a **machine-check result**. A machine may check that a document has the required fields, that a finite derivation uses the stated premises, or that a particular counterfactual experiment met a stated comparison. None of those facts alone establishes that its interpretation of explanation, reason use, authorship, progress, or capacity is correct.

Let \(A\) be the interpretation of this authority and \(D\) a dossier or implementation state. A proposed refinement provides a partial interpretation relation \(\mathcal I(D,A)\), rather than defining \(A\) to be whatever the implementation accepts. For every projected claim it states the subject, scope, interpretation, dependencies, and whether the projection concerns a record fact, a situated judgment, or semantic satisfaction. A machine-level classification may entail a semantic proposition only under an explicit bridge whose adequacy is itself open to criticism. Such a bridge may be an explanatory argument rather than a decision procedure.

A restrictive profile can require an explicit graph, an intervention family, a finite event vocabulary, a particular argument policy, or a designated witness format for its own checks. Failure to satisfy that profile means failure of that check or lack of evidence through that route. It does not, without a further argument, mean absence of the semantic phenomenon. Conversely, passing a profile is not a sufficient definition of that phenomenon. The profile’s restrictions and unsupported cases must remain visible; a restriction that excludes a genuine case cannot be hidden by renaming the case.

The connection must be substantive in both directions. An admissible encoding must explain what its positive classifications assert and why their reasons concern the attributed organization. It must also test characteristic legitimate cases against unintended exclusion, including inherited targets, communicated reconstruction, inexplicit contributions, method change, nonreplacement results, and provisional closure. This is not a demand that every finite implementation represent every possible history or that an entire semantic domain be enumerated.

Admission, use, and truth are distinct. Recording a proposed case does not make its body satisfy its own declared checks. Satisfying those checks does not make its premises true. A mechanically failed body must not be reported as having passed merely because nobody has objected to it. A different, adequately interpreted explanation can nevertheless bear on the same substantive claim.

Noninductivism forbids manufacturing explanatory warrant from a count of endorsements or repetitions. It does not make different arguments, different experimental results, or new information about a premise irrelevant merely because they have the same conclusion. An economical summary may ignore duplicate counts while the inquiry remains sensitive to the contents and dependencies of genuinely different reasons.

Operational stopping and epistemic immunity are different. An episode may close provisionally, an investigator may decline to pursue a criticism, an archive may stop accepting entries, and a physical system may cease to exist. None makes a content true or beyond criticism. Conversely, an external recorder’s ability to append objections does not establish the target’s recursive capacity. That attribution requires an account of the continuing organization’s ability to let scrutiny change its operative methods and judgments.

A specification may itself be criticized through the inquiry it supports. A replacement can be enacted by a person, institution, or software mechanism without requiring a currently executing evaluator to rewrite itself. The explanatory account must identify the criticism, proposed replacement, decision or action, and effects. Version labels alone establish traceability, not the return relevance of the criticism.

## Further discriminating cases

**Delayed reconstruction.** At \(e_1\), a relay emits a received explanation without constructing or reconstructing its explanatory organization. At \(e_2\), the continuing system develops that understanding through its own explanatory work. Authorship at \(e_2\) cannot combine with novelty and attempted use at \(e_1\) to establish OCA at \(e_1\). At \(e_2\), newness concerns earlier possessed understanding, not the earlier existence of the received string.

**Inherited target, original criticism.** An investigator inherits a theory, notices a defect, and creates an explanation of why the defect occurs. An appropriately connected creative critical episode is possible even though the investigator did not originate the theory and the new explanation is not its content descendant. A definition requiring that lineage excludes this case.

**Ordinary premise under criticism.** A criticism depends essentially on an observation interpretation rather than on a separately labeled standard. Withdrawing that interpretation in its relevant use removes that application’s support, just as withdrawing a standard would. It neither refutes the criticism’s conclusion automatically nor reinstates the criticized theory automatically.

**Same target, different achievement.** A tutor supplies a complete explanation and the learner genuinely reconstructs it. That can create explanatory knowledge for the learner. It does not by itself establish the learner’s capacity to originate that explanation in a challenge whose achievement condition excludes a supplied solution. Both classifications respect the same creative-reconstruction account.
