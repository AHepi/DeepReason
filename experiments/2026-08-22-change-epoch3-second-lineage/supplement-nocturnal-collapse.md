# Field-campaign notes: measuring the urban-rural air temperature contrast

Instrumentation and sampling procedure from a mobile-transect campaign.
These are observational protocols. They contain no mechanism and no
explanation; accounting for what they record is the question, not the data.

1. Sensors. Screen-level air temperature is logged at 2 m in aspirated
   radiation shields at 1 Hz. Unaspirated shields were rejected after a
   calibration round showed drift under low ventilation.

2. Transects. A fixed 42 km route crosses the built area and reaches open
   country at both ends. Each transect is driven twice, out and back, and
   the two passes are averaged so that the linear time trend cancels.

3. Timing. Runs begin 90 minutes past local sunset and finish before 03:00
   local time, the window in which the contrast is most stable.

4. Reference pairs. Every urban logger is paired with a rural logger at
   matched elevation (within 15 m) and matched distance from open water
   (within 2 km), so that terrain and coastline do not enter the contrast.

5. Weather screening. Each transect records 10 m wind speed and total cloud
   fraction from the nearest synoptic station at the transect midpoint.
   Transects are filed under three regimes -- calm-clear, intermediate, and
   windy-or-overcast -- by those two numbers alone.

6. Rejected data. Transects with precipitation at any point, or with a
   station gap exceeding 20 minutes, are discarded rather than
   interpolated.

7. Scope. The campaign records contrasts and the conditions under which
   they were measured. It ranks no city against another and proposes no
   cause.
