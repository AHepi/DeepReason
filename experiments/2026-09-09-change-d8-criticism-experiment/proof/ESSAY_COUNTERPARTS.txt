=== Step 16 proof: the bare-essay counterpart rule (R19 / SPEC S8 §10) ===

This rule was written THREE times. Each correction came from its own
self-test, before the pre-registration seals it -- which is the only
reason the order 'build, test, then seal' exists.

--- version 1: first prefix at or above the target, paragraph units ---
  achieved ratios 1.73 / 1.84 / 1.94 -- EVERY pair unmatched. Paragraphs
  run ~350 chars, so the crossing paragraph overshoots a ~1200-char
  position.

--- version 2: CLOSEST paragraph prefix, two-sided [1/1.5, 1.5] ---
  Still empty, and measurably so. The first committed essay's paragraph
  prefix lengths are:
    358, 464, 469, 579, 741, 2212, 2339, 2553, ...
  The window a 1200-char position allows is [800, 1800].
  paragraph prefixes inside that window: NONE
  sentence  prefixes inside that window: 888, 1079, 1082, 1322, 1494, 1497
  The granularity of a paragraph is too coarse for the window the length
  rule leaves. That is a fact about these essays, measured, not a guess.

--- version 3 (the rule, as it will be sealed): SENTENCE prefixes ---
three committed D8 essays: [7262, 7688, 7985] characters
  call-1: 19 paragraphs, 56 sentences
  call-2: 22 paragraphs, 53 sentences

108 pairs = 36 positions x 3 essays, and all 108 fall inside the length

versions of this rule were not, and which is the property that matters,
because the primary comparison is expected to be NULLed by length.

TWO BOUNDARIES THE RULE MUST NOT PAPER OVER, both asserted in the
self-test above: a target no prefix can reach reports UNMATCHED with its
shortfall stated rather than padding the essay; and a target far below
one sentence reports UNMATCHED rather than passing a one-sided test. The
two-sidedness is the load-bearing half: a one-sided rule would have let a
fragment a tenth the length count as 'within the length rule', smuggling
the length confound back in under the rule written to exclude it.
