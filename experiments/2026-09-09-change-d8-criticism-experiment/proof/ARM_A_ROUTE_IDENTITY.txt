R35 requires ARM A's generating seats to be byte-identical to ARM C's, and
R7 requires ARM V to differ only in the critic's endpoint. Three
hand-maintained YAML files would satisfy that on the day they were written
and drift the first time one was edited. One base table plus named
deviations makes the identity a property of the code.

Read the rows: every generating role has C==A true. Only 'judge' has
C==A false (ARM A's ensemble) and only 'argumentative_critic' has C==V
false (ARM V's stub). VIOLATIONS is empty. The comparison is on route
FINGERPRINTS, not on the YAML text: two files can differ in comments and
compile to the same routes, and it is the compiled routes the seats get.

--- A TRAP THIS STEP HIT, and it fails SILENTLY ---
The first compile passed single_model='qwen3.5:397b', copying the
reach-rich builder. ARM A came back with judge_routes=1. The parameter
collapses the role matrix to the one route carrying that model, which
DELETES the second judge seat -- and the compile succeeds either way, so
nothing says so. ARM A would have launched looking configured for a trial
and been unable to hold one, and the arm would have measured nothing while
appearing to work. The P-R1 tranche's own builder records the same trap in
the same words: 'the seats are cross-family, so single_model must be None'.

--- step 28: the authority reaches the seat, and the ensemble exists ---
ARM C: authority observe_only at the seat, gate False, ensemble REFUSED
ARM V: authority observe_only at the seat, gate False, ensemble REFUSED
ARM A: authority trial_required at the seat, gate True, ensemble OBTAINED,
       2 seats, families ['glm','qwen'], single_family_run False

ARM C IS THE RED CONTROL, and it is a natural one rather than a planted
one: it is single-family, so require_cross_family_judge_ensemble refuses
it. If ARM C also obtained an ensemble, ARM A's success would prove
nothing. The check asserts BOTH directions and fails on either.

criticism_policy is None in all three arms, which is what lets a
Config-side authority mode work at all: a manifest-bound policy call
refuses any mode outside {observe_only, defended_trial}.

--- step 29: what a defended trial ACTUALLY requires, answered from code ---
The question the spec could not settle by reading was whether
JUDGE_SEATS_ENABLED and a permissive rubric_policy are needed. Both
halves now have an answer:

  JUDGE_SEATS_ENABLED: REQUIRED. scheduler.py:1450 --
                if not self._judge_ensemble_available() or not config.JUDGE_SEATS_ENABLED:
                    continue
  and again at scheduler.py:2833 for the audit path. ARM A sets it true;
  ARM C and ARM V leave it false, which is the shipped default.

  rubric_policy: NOT REQUIRED, and not involved. grep for it in
  scheduler.py and informal/trial.py returns NOTHING -- it governs the
  normative-rubric surface, a different instrument from the defended
  trial. All three arms compile under rubric_policy='forbid', and ARM A's
  ensemble is obtainable under it, which is the measurement above.

  The trial additionally needs the roles argumentative_critic, defender
  and judge (informal/trial.py:569); all three arms populate all eleven
  canonical roles, so none is missing.
