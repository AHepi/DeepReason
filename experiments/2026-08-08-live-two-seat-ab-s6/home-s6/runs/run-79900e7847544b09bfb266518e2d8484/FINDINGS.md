# Findings

## Question

When a research team splits work across two different reasoning engines with different training histories, the SAME engine that authors a claim is often trusted to also check it. Argue for or against the claim that separating the authoring engine from the checking engine is necessary for a result to count as independently verified, and identify what evidence within a single run's own record would distinguish genuine independent verification from mere restatement. Where a claim can be tested by counting or by execution, say exactly what to count or execute.

## Positions the record accepts

- Separating authoring from checking is causally necessary for independent verification: a single engine cannot coherently be the author and the independent verifier of its own claim, because the verification act must be counterfactually sensitive to the claim being false, and an engine that generated the claim has, by construction, already committed to a probability mass in which the claim is true. `[6b6ba425f7df]`
- Separation of authoring and checking is not strictly necessary; what is necessary is that the verification procedure is mechanistically capable of falsifying the claim. Genuine verification is distinguished from restatement by whether the checker performs an operation that could have produced a negative result. If a same-engine check applies a deterministic falsification test (e.g., re-executing a claimed computation and comparing outputs), it constitutes genuine verification even without engine separation. `[de1003fa36f2]`
- Genuine independent verification within a single run's record is distinguished by the presence of a discriminating operation: an execution, a count, or a transformation whose output is not derivable from the claim's surface form. Restatement is identified by the absence of any such operation — the checking passage merely paraphrases the claim, applies no function, and introduces no new information. The definitive test is whether removing the claim from the checker's input would change the checker's output; if not, it is restatement. `[51f65c8e1292]`
